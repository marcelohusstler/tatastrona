Guide

How to use Fakturka and what you need to know about invoicing in Germany – short and in plain English.

Important: this is general information (as of November 2026), not tax advice. Rules change and every business is different – for individual questions, ask your tax advisor (Steuerberater).
Part 1 · The app, step by step

1. Open it and install it on your phone

Fakturka runs in your browser – nothing to download from an app store. Open the app, then add it to your home screen so it gets an icon like a normal app:

  • iPhone (Safari): tap Share (the square with an arrow) → Add to Home Screen → Add.
  • Android (Chrome): tap ⋮ in the top right → Add to Home screen or Install app.
  • Computer: just open the page. In Chrome you can also click the install icon in the address bar.
💡 Your data is saved in the browser you use. Invoices created on your phone won’t appear on your computer by themselves – use a backup to move them.

2. Set up your business (setup guide)

The first time you open the app, a short guide asks you step by step for:

  1. App language – English, German, Polish, Turkish, Russian, Italian or French.
  2. Document language – for customers in Germany, choose German. You can change it for each document. Note: changing the language does not translate items already added to a document – add them again from the template.
  3. Your trade – you get ready-made items (e.g. “wall painting per m²”).
  4. Company details – your name and full address are mandatory on an invoice.
  5. Taxes – whether you are a Kleinunternehmer, and your tax number (Steuernummer).
  6. Bank account – your IBAN appears in the invoice footer.
  7. Logo – optional, but it looks professional.

You can change everything later in the Company tab, where you can also restart the guide.

3. Your first quote (Angebot)

  1. In Documents, tap + New quote.
  2. Choose a customer or add a new one (+ New customer…). Mark whether it is a business or a private person.
  3. Tap + From template / catalogue and tap the items you want to add. Then tap Done.
  4. Enter quantities (e.g. 64 m²) and adjust prices. Totals, VAT and labour costs are calculated automatically.
  5. At the bottom you can edit the introduction and closing text.
  6. Tap Preview / PDF.

Everything is saved automatically – there is no “Save” button.

4. From quote to invoice (Rechnung)

When the customer accepts, open the quote and tap Convert to invoice. A new invoice is created with the same items and a reference to the quote number. Fill in the date of service (Leistungsdatum) – it is mandatory.

Once sent, set the status to Sent – unpaid, and after payment to Paid. Your document list shows what is outstanding and what is overdue.

5. PDF and sending it to your customer

In the preview, tap Print / Save PDF. The file is named after the invoice number and customer. Then:

  • iPhone: in the print dialog tap the Share icon at the top → Save to Files, or send it straight via WhatsApp or Mail.
  • Android: choose Save as PDF as the printer → tap the PDF icon → the file goes to Downloads.
  • Computer: choose Save as PDF as the printer.
💡 If you see a red “Check before sending” box above the preview, fill in what’s missing – for example your tax number or IBAN.

6. Your own items and prices

In the Items tab you build your own price list. You can:

  • copy items from a trade template (one at a time or the whole trade) and set your own prices,
  • add a completely new item (+ New),
  • while writing an invoice, tap ☆ Save to my items on any line.

Each item has a type: Labour, Material or Other. This matters because private customers can deduct labour costs from their tax – see §35a.

7. Company logo

  • Have a logo file (from a designer or your website)? Go to Company → Choose logo image.
  • No file? Take a photo of your logo on a business card, van or letterhead – on a white background, in good light. Or take a screenshot from your website or Facebook page and crop it in your gallery.

The app trims white margins around the logo automatically.

8. Backup

Your data lives only on your device. If you lose your phone or clear your browser, it is gone. So:

  1. Once a month: Company → Backup → Download backup. Save the file to Google Drive or iCloud, or email it to yourself.
  2. On a new phone or computer: Company → Load backup and pick that file.
Invoices must be kept for years (see below). Also save every PDF you send in a safe place – the app on your phone alone is not enough.
Part 2 · Invoicing in Germany

What must be on an invoice (Pflichtangaben)

The German VAT Act (§ 14 (4) UStG) lists the details an invoice must contain. If one is missing, a business customer may be unable to reclaim VAT and will ask you for a corrected invoice.

WhatIn German
Full name and address of your business and of the customerName und Anschrift
Your tax number or VAT IDSteuernummer / USt-IdNr.
Invoice dateRechnungsdatum
A sequential, unique invoice numberRechnungsnummer
Quantity and type of goods, or scope and type of serviceMenge und Art der Leistung
Date (or period) of the serviceLeistungsdatum / Leistungszeitraum
Net amount per VAT rate, plus any agreed discounts (e.g. Skonto)Entgelt
VAT rate and VAT amount, or the reason no VAT is chargedSteuersatz und Steuerbetrag
For work on a building or plot for a private person: a note that the customer must keep the invoice for 2 yearsAufbewahrungshinweis

Fakturka prints these details automatically and warns you in the preview if any of your data is missing.

Small invoices up to €250 gross (Kleinbetragsrechnung) can be simplified: your business name and address, the date, a description of the service and the gross amount with the VAT rate are enough. This does not apply to reverse charge, for example.

When you must issue an invoice

  • Business customer: you must issue the invoice within 6 months of the service.
  • Private customer + work on a building or plot (renovation, painting, garden, installations): an invoice within 6 months is also required – and it must include the 2-year retention note. In Fakturka that is one checkbox.
  • Other services for private persons: an invoice is not always mandatory, but customers usually want one – not least for the §35a deduction.

Kleinunternehmer – invoicing without VAT

A small business can use the small-business scheme (§ 19 UStG) and not charge VAT. Since 2025 the turnover limits are:

  • previous year: no more than €25,000,
  • current year: no more than €100,000. This limit applies immediately – the invoice that takes you over it must already include VAT.

In the year you start your business, the limit is €25,000. What counts is turnover (revenue), not profit.

A Kleinunternehmer shows no VAT on the invoice and adds a note about the exemption instead, for example:

Gemäß § 19 UStG wird keine Umsatzsteuer berechnet.

In Fakturka, just tick “I am a Kleinunternehmer” in the Company tab – the note is added automatically.

Note: if you opt out of this scheme, you are bound for 5 years. You choose your status when registering with the tax office (Finanzamt) – discuss it with a tax advisor.

Construction reverse charge (§ 13b UStG)

If you carry out construction work (e.g. as a subcontractor) for a business that itself provides construction services, it is your customer – not you – who accounts for the VAT. In that case:

  • you issue the invoice without VAT (net amount only),
  • you add a note that the tax liability is transferred,
  • you include the customer’s VAT ID (if they have one).

Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers gemäß § 13b UStG.

Customers usually confirm their status with a certificate from their tax office (form USt 1 TG) – ask for a copy and keep it. In Fakturka, choose the VAT rate 0% – Reverse charge §13b.

Reverse charge does not apply to private customers or to businesses that are not in construction (e.g. an office that orders painting). If you get it wrong, you may end up owing the VAT yourself – if in doubt, ask a tax advisor.

§ 35a – the argument that sells

Private customers can deduct part of the labour costs for work in their home from their income tax:

  • trade services (renovation, repairs, painting, installations): 20% of labour costs, up to €1,200 per year,
  • household services (e.g. cleaning, gardening): 20%, up to €4,000 per year.

Conditions: the customer has an invoice that shows labour separately and paid by bank transfer (not cash). Labour, travel and machine costs count – materials do not.

💡 If you want travel costs to be shown as deductible too, set that item’s type to “Labour”.

Fakturka calculates labour (items of type “Labour”) and prints it separately. Tell your customers – “you can deduct part of this from your taxes” is a strong argument when quoting.

Angebot or Kostenvoranschlag?

  • Angebot (quote) – usually binds you to the stated price once the customer accepts. Set a validity date (Fakturka uses 30 days by default).
  • Kostenvoranschlag (estimate) – an estimate of costs. If it will be exceeded significantly, you must tell the customer promptly, and they may cancel.

If you are unsure of the exact scope, say so clearly in the quote (e.g. that quantities will be billed according to the actual measurement – “Abrechnung nach Aufmaß”).

E-invoicing (E-Rechnung) 2025–2028

Germany is introducing mandatory e-invoices between businesses. An e-invoice is not a PDF but a file in a structured format (XRechnung or ZUGFeRD) that the customer’s software can read automatically.

FromWhat
1 Jan 2025Every business must be able to receive e-invoices (an email address is enough).
1 Jan 2027Businesses with more than €800,000 turnover in the previous year must send e-invoices to other businesses.
1 Jan 2028The obligation to send applies to all businesses for invoices to other businesses.

Not affected by the sending obligation: invoices to private customers, small invoices up to €250, and Kleinunternehmer (they only need to be able to receive).

Fakturka currently creates normal PDFs. XRechnung/ZUGFeRD support is planned before 2028.

Mistake on an invoice – what to do

  • Don’t quietly change an invoice the customer has already received, and never reuse an invoice number.
  • Issue a corrected invoice or a cancellation invoice (Stornorechnung) referring to the wrong invoice’s number, then a new, correct invoice with a new number.
  • Minor gaps (e.g. a missing date of service) can be fixed with a correction document that clearly states which invoice it refers to.

Automatic cancellation invoices are on Fakturka’s roadmap. Until then: duplicate the wrong invoice, title it “Stornorechnung zu RE-…” and enter the amounts as negative.

How long to keep invoices

Invoices (issued and received) must be kept for 8 years – this has applied since 2025 (it used to be 10). The period runs from the end of the year in which the invoice was issued. Files must remain readable for the whole period and must not be altered.

In practice: save every PDF you send in one cloud folder (e.g. “Rechnungen 2026”) and make regular backups of the app.
